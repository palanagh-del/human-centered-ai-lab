# Human-Centered AI Lab

This repository documents my learning and preparation in AI, HCI, and research-oriented technical work.

## Focus areas

I am exploring AI interpretability, AI safety, reasoning in AI systems, information retrieval, human-computer interaction, and human-centered AI evaluation.

## Why this repository exists

I am interested in how AI systems can become more understandable, reliable, and useful for people.

My background is in personal finance journalism, where I have seen how difficult complex information can be for ordinary users. This has shaped my interest in AI systems that explain clearly, communicate uncertainty, and support better human decision-making.

## Current learning goals

I am working on Python fundamentals, basic machine learning concepts, AI research papers, AI interpretability, AI safety, information retrieval, RAG systems, and HCI-based AI evaluation.

## Planned work

This repository will include research notes, paper summaries, small Python experiments, AI evaluation checklists, HCI reflections, and mini-projects on AI explanation and retrieval.

## First project direction

My first project direction is to study how AI-generated explanations can be evaluated across clarity, accuracy, reasoning quality, risk awareness, and user understanding.

## Long-term goal

My long-term goal is to build and study human-centered AI systems that are interpretable, safe, and useful in real-world decision-making contexts.
## SOAR preparation focus

As part of my preparation for open AI research opportunities such as EleutherAI's Summer of Open AI Research, I am using this repository to document my learning in areas that connect technical AI research with human understanding.

My current focus is on:

- information retrieval and RAG systems
- reasoning and evaluation in AI systems
- interpretability and AI safety
- human-centered evaluation of AI-generated explanations
- how users understand, trust, or question AI outputs

I am especially interested in how AI systems can provide answers that are not only correct, but also understandable, well-grounded, uncertainty-aware, and useful for real decision-making.
