Title: Learning note: mental models, RAG, and user trust

I read the Google People + AI Guidebook chapter on mental models and connected it to my interest in open AI research, RAG evaluation, and human-centered AI.

Main takeaway:
AI systems should not only be evaluated on whether they produce correct answers. They should also be evaluated on whether users correctly understand what kind of answer they are receiving.

Example:
A RAG-based finance assistant may retrieve general educational content about an equity fund. The answer may be accurate and sourced. But the user may think the system is giving personalised investment advice.

That creates a mismatch between system capability and user mental model.

This connects to larger questions in AI safety and evaluation:
- How do users interpret AI-generated answers?
- When do users over-trust a system?
- How can interfaces show limitations clearly?
- How can RAG systems communicate the difference between retrieved information and personalised judgement?

Possible research question:
How can AI interfaces help users distinguish between general information, explanation, and personalised advice in high-stakes domains?

Possible evaluation idea:
Compare a basic RAG answer with a mental-model-aware answer that includes sources, limitations, missing-information checklist, risk note, and clarifying questions.

My takeaway:
AI evaluation should include user understanding, trust calibration, and interface-level safety — not only technical accuracy.
